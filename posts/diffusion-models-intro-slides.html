    References and reading guide¶

    The section numbers below refer to this webpage (1–15); the images retain the original deck's page numbers (02–16).

    Flow Matching and continuous normalizing flows¶

    • Start here: Lipman et al. (2024), Flow Matching Guide and Code. A comprehensive guide to the mathematical foundations, design choices, and implementation of Flow Matching. Relevant throughout sections 1–7 and 14–15.
    • Lipman et al. (2022), Flow Matching for Generative Modeling. The main source for sections 4–6: conditional paths, the marginal velocity, and the equivalence of FM and CFM training gradients. See Sections 3–4 and Appendix B for the continuity equation.
    • Chen et al. (2018), Neural Ordinary Differential Equations. Background for the continuous normalizing flow construction in section 1.
    • Grathwohl et al. (2018), FFJORD: Free-form Continuous Dynamics for Scalable Reversible Generative Models. Background for likelihood-based CNF training and Jacobian-trace estimation in section 1.

    Diffusion, scores, and ODE/SDE sampling¶

    • Ho, Jain, and Abbeel (2020), Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models. Gaussian noising and denoising regression underlying section 9.
    • Song et al. (2021), Score-Based Generative Modeling through Stochastic Differential Equations. The main source for sections 2 and 9–10: score estimation, the reverse-time SDE, and the probability-flow ODE. See Sections 3.2–3.3, Section 4.3, and Appendix D.1. Equality of ODE/SDE time marginals assumes exact fields, matching initial laws, and well-posed evolution; it does not assert equality of individual trajectories.

    Conditioning and guidance¶

    • Ho and Salimans (2022), Classifier-Free Diffusion Guidance. Conditional/unconditional prediction mixing underlying sections 11–12. The slides use a scale convention where 0 is unconditional and 1 is conditional; compare conventions when reading the paper. The density reweighting illustration is not a guarantee of the guided sampler's marginals.

    Learning finite-interval maps and representations¶

    These papers correspond to section 13. They address different training objectives; representation learning alone does not establish one-step sampling.

    • Geng et al. (2025), Mean Flows for One-step Generative Modeling. Average velocity over a finite interval and its relation to instantaneous velocity.
    • Geng et al. (2025), Improved Mean Flows: On the Challenges of Fastforward Generative Models. iMF's reformulated training objective and explicit guidance conditioning.
    • Li and Zhu (2026), FlowMap-OPD: Rollout–Kernel Separation for On-Policy Distillation of Few-Step Flow-Map Generators. Separating student rollout states from teacher–student distribution comparisons.
    • Deng et al. (2026), Generative Modeling via Drifting. Evolving the generated distribution during training to obtain a generator with one-step inference.
    • Chefer et al. (2026), Self-Supervised Flow Matching for Scalable Multi-Modal Synthesis. Self-Flow's representation-learning approach and dual-timestep scheduling; the authors' project page links the paper and code.
